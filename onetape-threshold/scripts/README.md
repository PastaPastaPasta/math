# Scripts

Python 3 standard library only (tested with Python 3.11 and 3.13). Run from this directory. None of these scripts is part of the kernel-checked results; they re-check the Lean data independently, replay proofs from Williams's text, and compute the numbers of the paper that are not in Lean.

| Script | What it does | Written from | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dag_check.py` | Exact re-implementation of `HD.dagCheck` and `HDS.dagCheck`, run on the node lists of `StairD{1,2,3}.lean`; prints nodes, speedup steps, root ratio c·φ, the starting exponent a₀ and an upper bound on the unfolded length (exact, and rounded up) | the Lean definitions | < 1 s |
| `abs_check.py` | Exact re-implementation of `HLM.absCheck`, run on the data of `Ceiling.lean`, and on two perturbations of it that should fail | the Lean definitions | < 1 s |
| `replay39.py` | Replays the 39-line proof `HY.cert39` and prints it; with `--log-one`, `--log-in` or `--log-sp`, in the variant in which the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 has output exactly 1, max(b, 1) or max(1, e); reports the slowdowns at which b_k would matter | Williams's rules (TOCT text) | < 1 s |
| `w13_25line.py` | Replays the 25-line proof printed in Williams's §7.2 under the formal rules, with outputs max(b, e), 1, max(b, 1) and max(1, e) for the new log n block, per transition, and compares the carried superscripts with Williams's default (calibration; a replay of a rounded display, not a proof); asserts that all four outputs give the same kernels and deviations; reads every printed transition literally against the relaxed calculus and asserts which ones no rule reproduces (rounding, at most 3·10⁻⁹, and the log n outputs of row D3); `--tex` prints the body of the paper's A.2 table | Williams's rules and printed proof | < 1 s |
| `inventory.py` | Replays every other displayed derivation of Williams's §7 (p. A:31 example, Lemma 7.7, Corollary 7.8, the proof of Theorem 7.5, the sketch of Theorem 7.9) at exact rational parameter values against `HY.Step` and its variants, lists the differences, and asserts every agree/differ outcome and every formula of paper Appendix A, the Section 6 example of a slowdown below Lemma 7.2's value that meets the relaxed bound, and a sample at which inequality (31) of Case 1 fails (492 assertions) | Williams's displays, transcribed from the rendered pages | < 1 s |
| `staircase.py` | The staircase recursion: levels at a given c, the bisection estimates c_h, an estimate of c*_stair from a bracket with certified verdicts, and r_d, √(1+1/d), 1+1/(d+1) | paper Section 7.2 | < 1 s each |
| `stair_cert.py` | Untrusted generator of staircase certificates (JSON) | the recursion's structure | about 40–55 s (d = 3) to several minutes (d = 1) |
| `tex2md.py` | Regenerates `../paper/paper.md` from `../paper/paper.tex` (needs pandoc; tested with 3.12) | — | < 1 s |

```sh
python3 w13_25line.py
```
Every step of Williams's printed 25-line proof (c = 1.297844, d = 1) is a rule of the formal calculus with its side conditions. The replay starts from the printed first line and carries its own computed lines: each parameter (s, x) is read off the printed next line, but the rule is applied to the computed previous line, so the reported deviations accumulate along the replay. Compared are the kernel exponents (time, and space on DTISP lines) and, for every block, max(guess, 1), because the display prints guesses below 1 as n^1 (the first block's computed guess is 0.999997582); output superscripts are not printed and not compared. The largest deviation from a printed number is about 1.023·10⁻⁸ (1.0230064·10⁻⁸, so ≤ 1.03·10⁻⁸) for kernel exponents and about 5.6·10⁻⁹ for guesses; a per-transition table lists them, and the printed existential guess drops at lines 12 and 22 (1.040108911 → 1.040108909, 1.040108908), which no merged rule does. The replay is run with outputs max(b, e), 1, max(b, 1) and max(1, e) for the new log n block; all give the same deviations and kernels, so the display does not decide between the readings. Under max(b, e) and max(b, 1) no Lemma 7.2 step has b_k as the strict maximum; under outputs 1 and max(1, e) the slowdowns to lines 10 and 23 have it, by less than 6·10⁻⁹. Under max(b, e) the log n blocks of lines 13, 16, 19 and 22 carry the output ≈ 1.04010891 (inherited from the existential block), where Williams's default for an omitted superscript (max{guess, 1}, p. A:5, read block-wise) is 1; these are the only carried superscripts that differ from the default by more than 10⁻⁷, and the kernels are the same under the output 1. All four outputs give the same kernels and deviations (asserted). The computed last line, 1.7519584642, is about 1.023·10⁻⁸ above the printed start 1.751958454 and rounds to 1.751958464: the two differ when rounded to nine decimal places, first in the eighth decimal place (asserted), and at c = 1.297844 the replay is not a strict normal-form proof: it is a replay of Williams's rounded display, agreeing with it to within 1.03·10⁻⁸, not a verified proof at that c. It also reads every printed transition literally (omitted outputs read block-wise, printed kernels) against the relaxed calculus `HL.RStep` and asserts which ones no rule reproduces: the slowdowns to lines 4, 7, 11, 20, 23 and 24 print a time below c·max(pin rest, b, time k) (line 6 → 7: c · 1.155762944 = 1.500000002292736 > 1.5), the existential output falls at lines 12 (1.040108911 → 1.040108909, Lemma 7.1 merged) and 22 (→ 1.040108908, Lemma 3.3 merged), lines 13, 15 and 18 fail by rounding of a − s or x, and the log n blocks of lines 13, 16, 19 and 22 have the default output 1 below max(b, e) (row D3); apart from D3 every gap is at most 3·10⁻⁹ (paper D1, D2).

Replays every other displayed derivation of Williams's §7 against `HY.Step` at exact rational parameter values satisfying Williams's side conditions, from the printed previous line and from the computed one, and ends with a summary of the transitions that differ, a comparison of the kernels across outputs, and `492 assertions hold` (a failed assertion stops it). Each transition carries the asserted outcome from the printed previous line (agrees or differs) and, where paper Appendix A states one, the asserted formula for what `HY.Step` gives. The output of the new log n block is max(o, e) only for the p. A:31 example, Lemma 7.7 (base case and inductive step k = 2, d = 1, 2) and the 0,1,0 and 0,0 slowdowns; max(o, e) and 1 for Corollary 7.8 and the proof of Theorem 7.5; max(o, e), 1, max(o, 1) and max(1, e) for [1,1,0,0], Case 1 and Case 2 of the sketch of Theorem 7.9; for every display replayed under several outputs the kernels are the same under each (asserted). The splice of Lemma 7.7's induction is not replayed. The p. A:31 example and Lemma 7.7 agree.

The proof of Theorem 7.5 is replayed at d = 1, c = 1.29, a = 1.7617, x = 0.2583 (G = x + d(a − 1) = 1.02), which satisfy the conditions printed on p. A:34 with the common ε = η − c²/G > 0 (c((d+1)(a−1) − x) = 1.631979 ≥ η − ε = 1.631470…), and both exponents to which Corollary 7.8 is applied are below η = 1.632911…; the final exponent is 1.697382 < 1.7617. Corollary 7.8 is not displayed as a derivation; the script constructs one and asserts its endpoint: at the first use it is the line printed there, exactly; from DTIME_1[n^A] under a block (∀ n)^1 for 399 sample values A in [1, η) (d = 1, c = 1.29) it is the corollary's endpoint, exactly; at the second use it is the corollary's endpoint scaled by G, (∃ n^G)^G (∀ log n)^ℓ DTISP[n^G, n^{G·d/(d+1)}] with ℓ = G (max(o, e)) or 1 (output 1), which is not the line printed at that application: Williams prints (∀ n), guess n instead of log n, whose block-wise default output 1 also differs from G under max(o, e) (paper D7, D9). From the printed base case DTIME_d[n^{(d+1)/(d+2)}] (d = 1, 2, 3) no rule applies after Lemma 7.1 (D6; that the printed endpoint is two relaxed steps from the printed start is the Lean lemma `HL.cor78_base_reach`).

`roots`: r₁ = 1.3009428844, r₂ = 1.1887894930, r₃ = 1.1372807069; the bisection estimates c₁ = 1.330277691377, 1.199453639736, 1.142696215782 and the roots of the cubics 8c³ − 2c² − 13c + 2, 36c³ − 9c² − 46c + 6, 96c³ − 20c² − 113c + 12. `levels 1.3092 1`: seven levels, c·g = 1.059405, …, 0.994144 (refutes at level 6). `appear 1 6`: c₁, …, c₆ ≈ 1.330278, 1.311714, 1.309609, 1.309287, 1.309228, 1.309215, and an assertion that at c_h − 2·10⁻⁶ the recursion reaches level h without refuting before it (`appear 2 6`: 1.199454, 1.192559, 1.191768, …; `appear 3 6`: 1.142696, 1.139159, 1.138747, …). Here c_h is where the predicate `exists_level` — "the recursion reaches level h, or refutes before reaching it" — changes from true to false under float bisection on [1 + 1/(4d), 1 + 1/d]; it is not "level h exists" when the recursion refutes early (at d = 1, c = 5/4 level 0 refutes, so `exists_level(1.25, 1, 2)` is true, although continuing would give a level-2 candidate r₂ = 42375/13639 > 2). `threshold d 60`: first the verdicts at the exact endpoints 1 + 1/(4d) and 1 + 1/d, certified on outward-rounded decimal enclosures (REF at 5/4, 9/8, 13/12; STOP at 2, 3/2, 4/3); then a bracket of width 10⁻⁶⁰ whose two ends, exact decimals, have opposite verdicts, each certified by decimal interval arithmetic with directed rounding, and the digits the two ends share: c*_stair(1) ≈ 1.3092117446776010510221902603497164170365731409176335370894…, c*_stair(2) ≈ 1.19159683545302011311694956315014944535226780478297581606997…, c*_stair(3) ≈ 1.13865239000087119328135666360349492168754860133162470872070…. These are numerical estimates: the bisection assumes that the verdict is monotone in c, which is not proved; without it the bracket locates a change of verdict, not the supremum.
