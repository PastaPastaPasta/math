# SAT on d-dimensional one-tape machines: in Williams's calculus as formalised, his conjecture fails and the threshold lies in a short interval

For a Lean formalisation of the alternation-trading calculus of R. Williams, "Alternation-Trading Proofs, Linear Programming, and Lower Bounds" (ACM TOCT 5(2), 2013), §7, for SAT on machines with random-access input, n^{o(1)} bits of random-access storage and one d-dimensional sequential work tape (DTIME_d), under the hypothesis NTIME[n] ⊆ DTIME_d[n^c]:

- **The conjecture fails for the formalised calculus.** There are strict normal-form proofs at c = 1.30921174 (d = 1), 1.1915968 (d = 2) and 1.1386523 (d = 3). Williams's Theorem 7.5 gives the exponents r_d (roots of a quintic: 1.30094, 1.18879, 1.13728), and after it he conjectures that they are optimal for alternation-trading proofs. Every real root of each quintic is below the respective c. The proofs also satisfy a stricter reading of the speedup lemma: kernel time at least the input superscript, and space at most time, on every line. The same certificates also give such proofs in every variant of the calculus in which the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 has an output ℓ(o, e) with 1 ≤ ℓ(o, e) ≤ max(o, e) (`conjecture_fails_O`; o is the kernel's input superscript, e its space), among them output exactly 1, max(o, 1) and max(1, e) (`conjecture_fails_one`, `conjecture_fails_in`, `conjecture_fails_sp`). A 39-line proof at c = 1.30095 > r₁ is checked separately.
- **Ceilings.** For every c ≥ 1.3094 (d = 1), c ≥ 1.192 (d = 2) and c ≥ 1.1392 (d = 3) there is no normal-form proof from any starting exponent a₀ > 0, strict or non-strict, in the formal calculus, nor a non-strict one in a relaxed calculus that bounds every output only from below, drops quantifier types, guess exponents and the ≥ 1 floors, and contains the two-new-block form of Lemma 3.3 (Williams's Speedup Rule 2 in the form of Lemma 3.3, `speedNew`, relaxed `speedN`). (The condition a₀ > 0 is needed: with s = 0 the relaxed rules admit the path `([], T 0) → ([0], S 0 0) → ([], T 0)` at every c.) Both statements also hold with the sound inclusion DTIME_d[t] ⊆ DTISP[t, t] added. Williams's Lemma 3.4, in the forms that follow from the hypothesis (time and space exponents ≥ c, read with the maximum of Lemma 7.2), is Lemma 7.2 followed by this inclusion with weakening, which the relaxed statement covers (`HL.RPathX`, part (1) of `HLM.ceilingX_d{1,2,3}`); in the best case (both exponents c) it is Lemma 7.2 followed by the exact inclusion `HY.Incl`, which the exact statement covers too (part (2)). The same interval therefore bounds the threshold of the calculus with the inclusion added (`threshold_sup`). A closed-form ceiling: for every real d > 0 and c ≥ 1 with c² ≥ 1 + 1/d there is no strict normal-form proof (`HL.no_nf`); for d ≥ 1 also not with the inclusion and with any output superscript ≥ 0 for the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 (`HL.no_drop_W`, which excludes a' < a₀, not a path with a' = a₀), in particular in every calculus `HY.StepO ℓ` of the previous item (`HL.no_nfO`). √(1 + 1/d) is below Williams's 1 + 1/(d+1) (Theorem 7.9, stated with a proof sketch) for every d ≥ 1, so for every integer dimension, though not for every real d > 0 (at d = 1/2, √3 > 5/3).
- **Scope of the ceilings.** They are theorems about the formalised rule set (`HY.Step`, paper Table 2, with the extensions named in each theorem). That rule set is our reading of Williams's lemmas, as set out in paper Section 2.4. His proof sketches display some lines that differ from it (Section 2.4, Appendix A), and for some of those the ceilings are not established. The scope table at the end of paper Section 2.4 is the one full statement of what is covered, and how (an exact step; a single relaxed step, one `HL.RStep` or `HL.Widen` step, covered by the relaxed ceilings; a single `HL.Loose` step, covered by the relaxed closed-form ceiling `HL.qf_ge_W` only; or an expansion into several steps)
; we do not claim its list is exhaustive.
- **Every displayed derivation step of Williams's §7 is compared with the formal rules** (paper Appendix A, `scripts/inventory.py`, `scripts/w13_25line.py`, which assert every outcome and formula stated there). Williams's 25-line proof as printed has transitions that no rule reproduces literally, even in the relaxed calculus: at c = 1.297844 the slowdown from kernel time 1.155762944 (line 6) to 1.5 (line 7), where every slowdown rule needs at least 1.297844 · 1.155762944 = 1.500000002292736, and, with omitted outputs read block-wise, the existential output falling from 1.040108911 to 1.040108909 at line 12, where Lemma 7.1 merged needs the new output ≥ the old (rows D1, D2; each such gap is at most 3·10⁻⁹, asserted by `w13_25line.py`). The printed numbers differ from the exact replay by at most 1.03·10⁻⁸ (kernels); the numerical origin of the discrepancies is not determined, and the comparison uses the exact replay in place of the printed numbers (at c = 1.29784399 that replay passes every side condition and ends at 1.751958432856…, strictly below its start 1.751958454; asserted). One line of the prose proof of Theorem 7.5 (p. A:34) keeps a DTISP kernel after a step labelled as a slowdown; for real d ≥ 0 and 1 ≤ c ≤ 1 + 1/(d+1) (Williams assumes c < (d+2)/(d+1) there) it is derived exactly by Lemma 7.2 followed by Lemma 7.1 (`HY.slow_convMerge_A34`), and for larger c its coverage is not established (row D8).
- **The existence results under the smaller superscripts.** Our derivations write max(o, e) for the output of the new log n block of Lemma 3.3; three displays in Williams's sketch of Theorem 7.9 show 1 or max(o, 1). Since raising an output superscript only enlarges a class, our derivations remain proofs of the lower bounds under every reading, but a derivation writing max(o, e) is not literally a derivation in a calculus whose exact output is smaller. For the three staircase certificates this is closed in Lean: the soundness proofs of both DAG checkers hold for every output ℓ(o, e) of that block with 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ max(o, e), so the same checks give strict normal-form proofs (kernel time ≥ input, space ≤ time) for each such output (`conjecture_fails_O`), in particular for 1, max(o, 1) and max(1, e) (`conjecture_fails_one`, `conjecture_fails_in`, `conjecture_fails_sp`). For the 39-line proof a script replays it with outputs 1, max(o, 1) and max(1, e), with every side condition passing and the same kernels (`replay39.py --log-one`, `--log-in`, `--log-sp`); this is not kernel-checked. Under these smaller outputs the sharp ceilings are not established in general (paper Section 2.4).
- **The threshold** (the supremum of the c with a proof; `threshold_sup`) therefore lies in [1.30921174, 1.3094], [1.1915968, 1.192] and [1.1386523, 1.1392]. **Its exact value is open.** A recursion read off from the shape of the best proofs found (a staircase of levels) has a boundary between refuting and stopping that we estimate numerically as c*_stair ≈ 1.3092117446776010510 for d = 1 (the estimate assumes the recursion's verdict is monotone in c, which is not proved); that the threshold of the calculus equals c*_stair is a conjecture supported by numerics only.

The existence results, the ceilings and the bounds on the threshold are kernel-checked in Lean for the *formal* calculus. The constructions are DAG certificates (shared sub-derivations reused at every scale) checked by a rational checker with a soundness theorem; unfolded, the d = 1 proof has at most 7.62·10²¹ lines, a constant independent of n. The ceilings are multi-level potential functions; their Lemma 7.1 condition is checked for all words at once by a finite abstraction.

What is not machine-checked: that the formal rules are Williams's (a modelling claim, compared rule by rule in the paper, with the reading choices of paper Section 2.4, whose scope table states what the ceilings cover, and display by display in Appendix A), and the soundness of Williams's lemmas. The complexity consequence, SAT ∉ DTIME_d[n^c] for every c below 1.30921174, 1.1915968 and 1.1386523, holds exactly as far as Williams's Lemmas 7.1, 7.2, 7.3, his Lemma 3.3 used inside a prefix, the combining of same-type blocks (his §2) and his Theorem 3.1 with Corollary 3.2 do; his Theorem 7.5 rests on the same lemmas. This is a statement about one proof calculus, not about lower bounds by other methods.
