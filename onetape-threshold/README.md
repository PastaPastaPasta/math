# SAT on d-dimensional one-tape machines: Williams's conjecture fails, and the calculus stops in a short interval

For a Lean formalisation of the alternation-trading calculus of R. Williams, "Alternation-Trading Proofs, Linear Programming, and Lower Bounds" (ACM TOCT 5(2), 2013), §7, for SAT on machines with random-access input, n^{o(1)} bits of random-access storage and one d-dimensional sequential work tape (DTIME_d), under the hypothesis NTIME[n] ⊆ DTIME_d[n^c]:

- **The conjecture fails for the formalised calculus.** There are strict normal-form proofs at c = 1.30921174 (d = 1), 1.1915968 (d = 2) and 1.1386523 (d = 3). Williams's Theorem 7.5 gives the exponents r_d (roots of a quintic: 1.30094, 1.18879, 1.13728), and after it he conjectures that they are optimal for alternation-trading proofs. Every real root of each quintic is below the respective c. The proofs also satisfy a stricter reading of the speedup lemma: kernel time at least the input superscript, and space at most time, on every line. A 39-line proof at c = 1.30095 > r₁ is checked separately.
- **Ceilings.** For every c ≥ 1.3094 (d = 1), c ≥ 1.192 (d = 2) and c ≥ 1.1392 (d = 3) there is no normal-form proof, strict or non-strict, in the formal calculus, nor a non-strict one in a relaxed calculus that bounds every output only from below, drops quantifier types, guess exponents and the ≥ 1 floors, and contains Williams's Speedup Rule 2. A closed-form ceiling: for every real d > 0 and c ≥ 1 with c² ≥ 1 + 1/d there is no strict normal-form proof; √(1 + 1/d) is below Williams's 1 + 1/(d+1) (Theorem 7.9) for every d ≥ 1.
- **The threshold** (the supremum of the c with a proof) therefore lies in [1.30921174, 1.3094], [1.1915968, 1.192] and [1.1386523, 1.1392]. **Its exact value is open.** A recursion read off from the shape of the optimal proofs (a staircase of levels) has threshold c*_stair = 1.3092117446776010510… for d = 1; that the threshold of the calculus equals c*_stair is a conjecture supported by numerics only.

All three items are kernel-checked in Lean for the *formal* calculus. The constructions are DAG certificates (shared sub-derivations reused at every scale) checked by a rational checker with a soundness theorem; unfolded, the d = 1 proof has up to about 7.6·10²¹ lines, a constant independent of n. The ceilings are multi-level potential functions; their Lemma 7.1 condition is checked for all words at once by a finite abstraction.

What is not machine-checked: that the formal rules are Williams's (a modelling claim, compared rule by rule in the paper, with the reading choices listed there: n^{o(1)} factors dropped, merging of same-type blocks, Lemma 3.3 inside a prefix under a literal and a strict reading, log n blocks in Lemma 7.2, strict versus non-strict normal form), and the soundness of Williams's lemmas. The complexity consequence, SAT ∉ DTIME_d[n^c] for every c below 1.30921174, 1.1915968 and 1.1386523, holds exactly as far as Williams's Lemmas 7.1, 7.2, 7.3, his Lemma 3.3 used inside a prefix and his Theorem 3.1 do; his Theorem 7.5 rests on the same lemmas. The relaxed calculus contains every move of Williams's calculus as we read it; one sound move outside the calculus, DTIME_d[t] ⊆ DTISP[t, t] for d ≥ 2, is not covered (paper Section 6). This is a statement about one proof calculus, not about lower bounds by other methods.
