# SAT on d-dimensional one-tape machines: in Williams's calculus as formalised, his conjecture fails and the threshold lies in a short interval

For a Lean formalisation of the alternation-trading calculus of R. Williams, "Alternation-Trading Proofs, Linear Programming, and Lower Bounds" (ACM TOCT 5(2), 2013), §7, for SAT on machines with random-access input, n^{o(1)} bits of random-access storage and one d-dimensional sequential work tape (DTIME_d), under the hypothesis NTIME[n] ⊆ DTIME_d[n^c]:

- **The conjecture fails for the formalised calculus.** There are strict normal-form proofs at c = 1.30921174 (d = 1), 1.1915968 (d = 2) and 1.1386523 (d = 3). Williams's Theorem 7.5 gives the exponents r_d (roots of a quintic: 1.30094, 1.18879, 1.13728), and after it he conjectures that they are optimal for alternation-trading proofs. Every real root of each quintic is below the respective c. The proofs also satisfy a stricter reading of the speedup lemma: kernel time at least the input superscript, and space at most time, on every line. The same certificates also give such proofs in every variant of the calculus in which the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 has an output ℓ(o, e) with 1 ≤ ℓ(o, e) ≤ max(o, e) (`conjecture_fails_O`; o is the kernel's input superscript, e its space), among them output exactly 1, max(o, 1) and max(1, e) (`conjecture_fails_one`, `conjecture_fails_in`, `conjecture_fails_sp`). A 39-line proof at c = 1.30095 > r₁ is checked separately.
- **Ceilings.** For every c ≥ 1.3094 (d = 1), c ≥ 1.192 (d = 2) and c ≥ 1.1392 (d = 3) there is no normal-form proof from any starting exponent a₀ > 0, strict or non-strict, in the formal calculus, nor a non-strict one in a relaxed calculus that bounds every output only from below, drops quantifier types, guess exponents and the ≥ 1 floors, and contains Williams's Speedup Rule 2. (The condition a₀ > 0 is needed: with s = 0 the relaxed rules admit the path `([], T 0) → ([0], S 0 0) → ([], T 0)` at every c.) Both statements also hold with the sound inclusion DTIME_d[t] ⊆ DTISP[t, t] added; Williams's Lemma 3.4, in the forms that follow from the hypothesis (time and space exponents ≥ c) and read with the maximum of Lemma 7.2, is Lemma 7.2 followed by this inclusion with weakening. The same interval therefore bounds the threshold of the calculus with the inclusion added. A closed-form ceiling: for every real d > 0 and c ≥ 1 with c² ≥ 1 + 1/d there is no strict normal-form proof; for d ≥ 1 also not with the inclusion and with any output superscript ≥ 0 for the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 (there it excludes a' < a₀, not a path with a' = a₀). These ceilings are theorems about the formalised rule set: the rules of `HY.Step` (paper Table 2) plus the extensions named in each theorem (`HL.RStep`, `HY.Incl`/`HL.Widen`, and for the closed-form ceiling `HY.SpeedLog`/`HL.Loose`). That rule set follows the statements of Williams's Lemmas 7.1, 3.3 and 7.2 with their side conditions. Williams's text differs from it in the places listed in paper Section 2.4 and Appendix A; the known differences that matter for the ceilings are: (a) the output max(o, e) of the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 versus the smaller superscripts displayed on pp. A:36–A:38 (sharp ceilings only); (b) the full maximum of Lemma 7.2, including b_k, versus the 0,1,0 and 0,0 displays on p. A:36; (c) both halves of the p. A:31 sentence "When C is DTISP, the Speedup and Slowdown Rules from Section 4 apply": a speedup of a DTISP kernel is read as Lemma 3.3 and a slowdown as Lemma 7.2 (or Lemma 3.4 with exponents ≥ c), and the literal Speedup Rules 0–2 and Slowdown Rule of §4 on a DTISP kernel (DTS outputs) are excluded, with nothing claimed for them; (d) the side conditions of Lemma 3.3, in particular e ≤ a − x, which the p. A:36 display (stating only x, y ≥ 0) does not impose; (e) no splitting of a block into two of the same type (alternation as in Definition 4.1). We do not claim this list of reading choices is exhaustive. √(1 + 1/d) is below Williams's 1 + 1/(d+1) (Theorem 7.9, stated with a proof sketch) for every d ≥ 1, so for every integer dimension, though not for every real d > 0 (at d = 1/2, √3 > 5/3).
- **Every displayed derivation step of Williams's §7 is compared with the formal rules** (paper Appendix A, `scripts/inventory.py`, `scripts/w13_25line.py`). Williams's 25-line proof as printed has transitions that no rule reproduces literally, even in the relaxed calculus: at c = 1.297844 the slowdown from kernel time 1.155762944 (line 6) to 1.5 (line 7), where every slowdown rule needs at least 1.297844 · 1.155762944 = 1.500000002292736, and, with omitted outputs read block-wise, the existential output falling from 1.040108911 to 1.040108909 at line 12, where Lemma 7.1 merged needs the new output ≥ the old (rows D1, D2; each such gap is at most 3·10⁻⁹, asserted by `w13_25line.py`). The printed numbers differ from the exact replay by at most 1.03·10⁻⁸ (kernels); the numerical origin of the discrepancies is not determined, and the comparison uses the exact replay in place of the printed numbers (at c = 1.29784399 that replay passes every side condition and ends at 1.751958432856…, strictly below its start 1.751958454; asserted). The known differences (a)–(e) listed in the previous item, in more detail. (a) Inside a prefix with input superscript o we give the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 the output superscript max(o, e), which is what the proof of Lemma 3.3 gives inside a prefix. Three displays in Williams's sketch of Theorem 7.9 show a smaller one: 1 (TOCT pp. A:36 and A:38), and max{b, 1} on p. A:37, where b is the input superscript of the kernel (the line there is Q^b (Q₁ n^{a′})^b DTISP[n^a, n^e]), so the output is max(o, 1) = o. The ceilings 1.3094, 1.192 and 1.1392 are proved for our reading only; under the displayed ones only the closed-form ceiling is proved, for strict normal-form proofs, and we do not know whether proofs at or above those values exist (paper Section 2.4, item 3). (b) Our slowdowns use the full maximum of Lemma 7.2, which contains the output b_k of the remaining prefix; two displays on p. A:36 omit it. The 0,1,0 display prints max{cb, ca − cs, ca′, ca, cds} where Lemma 7.2 gives c·max(b′, b, a − s, a′, a): it omits c·b′ and adds c·d·s. The 0,0 comparison display prints max{cb, ca, ca′} where Lemma 7.2 gives c·max(b′, b, a′, a): it omits c·b′ and has no d·s term. The 0,1,0 display is below Lemma 7.2's value exactly when b′ > max{b, a − s, a′, a, d·s} (d = 1, c = 3/2: 45/4 instead of 15), the 0,0 display exactly when b′ > max{b, a′, a}; for these two displays this is also exactly when they are below the bound c·max(b_k, b_{k+1}, a_{k+1}) of the relaxed slowdown, and then no ceiling covers them. That b′ is the strict maximum of Lemma 7.2 is not enough (d = 2, c = 3/2, a = s = 2, b′ = 3, b = a′ = 1: 6 against 9/2). The 0,1,0 display can also be above Lemma 7.2's value for d ≥ 2 (d = 2, c = 3/2: 6 instead of 3), which the relaxed slowdown covers as a weakening (item 5). (In general the relaxed slowdown drops the removed block's guess, so a slowdown below Lemma 7.2's value can still be a relaxed step; paper Section 6.) (c) P. A:31 says that for a DTISP kernel "the Speedup and Slowdown Rules from Section 4 apply", and its list of rules names Lemma 3.3 for speedups and Lemma 3.4 for slowdowns of a DTISP class. We read speedups of a DTISP kernel as Lemma 3.3 (guess n^{x+e}, first-block output max(b, x + e), log n output max(b, e), kernel DTISP[n^{a−x}, n^e], condition e ≤ a − x) and slowdowns as Lemma 7.2 (or Lemma 3.4 with exponents ≥ c). The literal Speedup Rules 0–2 and Slowdown Rule of §4 applied to a DTISP kernel (DTS output, i.e. space n^{o(1)}; guess n^x; outputs max{x, b} and b; no condition e ≤ a − x) are excluded, and nothing is claimed for them (item 7). (d) The display for the annotation [1,1,0,0] on p. A:36 is stated for x, y ≥ 0 without Lemma 3.3's condition e ≤ a − x: at d = 1, a = 4, x = 3, y = 2 it goes from DTISP[n^4, n^3] to DTISP[n^2, n^3], a step of no exact or relaxed rule (row D17; not covered). (e) No rule splits a block into two of the same type; the exact lines alternate as in Definition 4.1. The misprinted base case of Corollary 7.8 (p. A:34, row D6) is not reached by the exact rules, but its printed endpoint is reached from its printed start by two relaxed steps (`HL.cor78_base_reach`), so the relaxed statements cover a proof that uses it as printed, from the empty prefix (inside a prefix this is not checked). One further display looked like another difference: the prose proof of Williams's Theorem 7.5 (p. A:34) writes a DTISP kernel, DTISP[n^c, n^{d/(d+1)}], after a step labelled as a slowdown. That line is derived exactly by Lemma 7.2 followed by Lemma 7.1 merged with s = 1/(d+1), since c − 1/(d+1) ≤ 1 for Williams's c < (d+2)/(d+1) (`HY.slow_convMerge_A34`, kernel-checked), so every result covers it (item 6, row D8). `scripts/inventory.py` asserts every agree/differ outcome and every formula of the appendix.
- **The existence results under the smaller superscripts.** Our derivations write max(o, e). Since raising an output superscript only enlarges a class, they remain proofs of the lower bounds under every reading, but a derivation writing max(o, e) is not literally a derivation in a calculus whose exact output is smaller. For the three staircase certificates this is closed in Lean: the soundness proofs of both DAG checkers hold for every output ℓ(o, e) of that block with 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ max(o, e), so the same checks give strict normal-form proofs (kernel time ≥ input, space ≤ time) for each such output (`conjecture_fails_O`), in particular for the displayed outputs 1 (`conjecture_fails_one`) and max(o, 1) (`conjecture_fails_in`). For the 39-line proof a script replays it with outputs 1, max(o, 1) and max(1, e), with every side condition passing and the same kernels (`replay39.py --log-one`, `--log-in`, `--log-sp`); this is not kernel-checked. Case 1 of the sketch (p. A:37) also prints the guess n^y where Lemma 3.3 gives n^{y+e}; our calculi keep Lemma 3.3's guess, so a calculus with that smaller guess is not checked (relaxed lines have no guesses: with the printed log n outputs max{b, 1} = b that step is covered by the relaxed parts of the sharp ceilings when e ≤ b, and when e > b only by the relaxed closed-form statement `HL.qf_ge_W`). For the slowdown without b_k: at no Lemma 7.2 step of the 39-line proof is b_k the strict maximum, nor in the replay of Williams's 25-line proof under the outputs max(o, e) and max(o, 1) (under the outputs 1 and max(1, e) it is, at two slowdowns, by less than 6·10⁻⁹) (scripts); for the staircase certificates this is not checked.
- **The threshold** (the supremum of the c with a proof) therefore lies in [1.30921174, 1.3094], [1.1915968, 1.192] and [1.1386523, 1.1392]. **Its exact value is open.** A recursion read off from the shape of the best proofs found (a staircase of levels) has a boundary between refuting and stopping that we estimate numerically as c*_stair ≈ 1.3092117446776010510 for d = 1 (the estimate assumes the recursion's verdict is monotone in c, which is not proved); that the threshold of the calculus equals c*_stair is a conjecture supported by numerics only.

The existence results, the ceilings and the bounds on the threshold are kernel-checked in Lean for the *formal* calculus. The constructions are DAG certificates (shared sub-derivations reused at every scale) checked by a rational checker with a soundness theorem; unfolded, the d = 1 proof has at most 7.62·10²¹ lines, a constant independent of n. The ceilings are multi-level potential functions; their Lemma 7.1 condition is checked for all words at once by a finite abstraction.

What is not machine-checked: that the formal rules are Williams's (a modelling claim, compared rule by rule in the paper, with the reading choices listed there: n^{o(1)} factors dropped, merging of same-type blocks, Lemma 3.3 inside a prefix under a literal and a strict reading, with its side conditions and with output max(o, e) for the new log n block, log n blocks in Lemma 7.2 and its full maximum including b_k, speedups and slowdowns of a DTISP kernel read as Lemma 3.3 and Lemma 7.2 with the literal rules of his §4 excluded, alternation without splitting of blocks, strict versus non-strict normal form; the list is not claimed to be exhaustive; and display by display in Appendix A), and the soundness of Williams's lemmas. The complexity consequence, SAT ∉ DTIME_d[n^c] for every c below 1.30921174, 1.1915968 and 1.1386523, holds exactly as far as Williams's Lemmas 7.1, 7.2, 7.3, his Lemma 3.3 used inside a prefix, the combining of same-type blocks (his §2) and his Theorem 3.1 with Corollary 3.2 do; his Theorem 7.5 rests on the same lemmas. The relaxed calculus contains every rule of `HY.Step` (`HL.sim`) but not the slowdowns by Lemma 3.4, which are covered once the inclusion is added: Williams lists "Slowdown via Lemma 3.4 when C is a DTISP class" among his rules (p. A:31); the forms of it that follow from the hypothesis, with time and space exponents ≥ c (best: both equal to c), read inside a prefix with the maximum of Lemma 7.2 (the §3 statement alone is for one block, with the three-term maximum max{a, a′, b}), are Lemma 7.2 followed by DTIME_d[t] ⊆ DTISP[t', e'] with t', e' ≥ t, and the ceilings cover that inclusion (paper Section 6); for space exponents below c we know no derivation. This is a statement about one proof calculus, not about lower bounds by other methods.
