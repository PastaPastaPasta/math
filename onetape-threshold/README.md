# SAT on d-dimensional one-tape machines: in Williams's calculus as formalised, his conjecture fails and the threshold lies in a short interval

For a Lean formalisation of the alternation-trading calculus of R. Williams, "Alternation-Trading Proofs, Linear Programming, and Lower Bounds" (ACM TOCT 5(2), 2013), §7, for SAT on machines with random-access input, n^{o(1)} bits of random-access storage and one d-dimensional sequential work tape (DTIME_d), under the hypothesis NTIME[n] ⊆ DTIME_d[n^c]:

- **The conjecture fails for the formalised calculus.** There are strict normal-form proofs at c = 1.30921174 (d = 1), 1.1915968 (d = 2) and 1.1386523 (d = 3). Williams's Theorem 7.5 gives the exponents r_d (roots of a quintic: 1.30094, 1.18879, 1.13728), and after it he conjectures that they are optimal for alternation-trading proofs. Every real root of each quintic is below the respective c. The proofs also satisfy a stricter reading of the speedup lemma: kernel time at least the input superscript, and space at most time, on every line. The same certificates also give such proofs in the variant of the calculus in which the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 has output exactly 1 (`conjecture_fails_one`). A 39-line proof at c = 1.30095 > r₁ is checked separately.
- **Ceilings.** For every c ≥ 1.3094 (d = 1), c ≥ 1.192 (d = 2) and c ≥ 1.1392 (d = 3) there is no normal-form proof from any starting exponent a₀ > 0, strict or non-strict, in the formal calculus, nor a non-strict one in a relaxed calculus that bounds every output only from below, drops quantifier types, guess exponents and the ≥ 1 floors, and contains Williams's Speedup Rule 2. (The condition a₀ > 0 is needed: with s = 0 the relaxed rules admit the path `([], T 0) → ([0], S 0 0) → ([], T 0)` at every c.) Both statements also hold with the sound inclusion DTIME_d[t] ⊆ DTISP[t, t] added; Williams's Lemma 3.4, in the forms that follow from the hypothesis (time and space exponents ≥ c), is Lemma 7.2 followed by this inclusion with weakening. A closed-form ceiling: for every real d > 0 and c ≥ 1 with c² ≥ 1 + 1/d there is no strict normal-form proof; for d ≥ 1 also not with the inclusion and with any output superscript for the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 (there it excludes a' < a₀, not a path with a' = a₀). √(1 + 1/d) is below Williams's 1 + 1/(d+1) (Theorem 7.9) for every d ≥ 1, so for every integer dimension, though not for every real d > 0 (at d = 1/2, √3 > 5/3).
- **Two readings matter for the ceilings.** First, inside a prefix with input superscript b we give the new log n block of Lemma 3.3 the output superscript max(b, e), which is what the proof of Lemma 3.3 gives inside a prefix (and Speedup Rule 1 of Williams's Definition 4.1 for DTS kernels). Three displays in Williams's sketch of Theorem 7.9 (TOCT pp. A:36, A:37 and A:38) show a smaller one (1, or max{b, 1}). The ceilings 1.3094, 1.192 and 1.1392 are proved for our reading only; under the displayed one only the closed-form ceiling is proved, for strict normal-form proofs, and we do not know whether proofs at or above those values exist (paper Section 2.4, item 3). Second, the ceilings take the output of Lemma 7.2 to be DTIME_d; the prose proof of Williams's Theorem 7.5 (p. A:34) shows one slowdown that keeps a DTISP kernel with space n^{d/(d+1)} below its time n^c, a line we know no way to derive from the hypothesis and that the ceilings do not cover (item 6).
- **The existence results under the smaller superscript.** Our derivations write max(b, e). Since raising an output superscript only enlarges a class, they remain proofs of the lower bounds under either reading, but a derivation writing max(b, e) is not literally a derivation in a calculus whose exact output is 1. For the three staircase certificates this is closed in Lean: the soundness proofs of both DAG checkers hold for every output ℓ(o, e) of that block with 1 ≤ ℓ ≤ max(o, e), so the same checks give strict normal-form proofs (kernel time ≥ input, space ≤ time) when the output is exactly 1 (`conjecture_fails_one`). For the 39-line proof a script replays it with output exactly 1, with every side condition passing and the same kernels (`replay39.py --log-one`); this is not kernel-checked. The output max{b, 1} of one display depends on an outer superscript; a calculus with that exact output is not checked.
- **The threshold** (the supremum of the c with a proof) therefore lies in [1.30921174, 1.3094], [1.1915968, 1.192] and [1.1386523, 1.1392]. **Its exact value is open.** A recursion read off from the shape of the best proofs found (a staircase of levels) has a boundary between refuting and stopping that we estimate numerically as c*_stair ≈ 1.3092117446776010510 for d = 1 (the estimate assumes the recursion's verdict is monotone in c, which is not proved); that the threshold of the calculus equals c*_stair is a conjecture supported by numerics only.

The existence results, the ceilings and the bounds on the threshold are kernel-checked in Lean for the *formal* calculus. The constructions are DAG certificates (shared sub-derivations reused at every scale) checked by a rational checker with a soundness theorem; unfolded, the d = 1 proof has at most 7.62·10²¹ lines, a constant independent of n. The ceilings are multi-level potential functions; their Lemma 7.1 condition is checked for all words at once by a finite abstraction.

What is not machine-checked: that the formal rules are Williams's (a modelling claim, compared rule by rule in the paper, with the reading choices listed there: n^{o(1)} factors dropped, merging of same-type blocks, Lemma 3.3 inside a prefix under a literal and a strict reading and with output max(b, e) for the new log n block, log n blocks in Lemma 7.2, the output of Lemma 7.2 always DTIME_d, strict versus non-strict normal form), and the soundness of Williams's lemmas. The complexity consequence, SAT ∉ DTIME_d[n^c] for every c below 1.30921174, 1.1915968 and 1.1386523, holds exactly as far as Williams's Lemmas 7.1, 7.2, 7.3, his Lemma 3.3 used inside a prefix and his Theorem 3.1 do; his Theorem 7.5 rests on the same lemmas. The relaxed calculus contains every move of Williams's calculus as we read it (with the two readings above). Williams also lists "Slowdown via Lemma 3.4 when C is a DTISP class" among his rules (p. A:31); the forms of it that follow from the hypothesis, with time and space exponents ≥ c (best: both equal to c), are Lemma 7.2 followed by DTIME_d[t] ⊆ DTISP[t', e'] with t', e' ≥ t, and the ceilings cover that inclusion (paper Section 6); for space exponents below c we know no derivation. This is a statement about one proof calculus, not about lower bounds by other methods.
